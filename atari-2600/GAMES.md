# Atari 2600 Jr.

Purchased at the game store.

# Game Suggestions from Copilot

🎮 Absolute Must‑Haves (Core 2600 Experience)

These are the cartridges that define the system — the ones every collection feels naked without.

🏆 Top‑Tier Essentials

    Pitfall! — The closest thing the 2600 has to a prestige platformer. Still fun, still tense.

    River Raid — Tight controls, great pacing, and one of the best‑designed shooters on the system.

    Adventure — The proto‑Zelda. Charming, weird, and historically important.

    Space Invaders — The killer app that made the 2600 explode in popularity.

    Asteroids — Surprisingly faithful and very replayable.

    Missile Command — Pure arcade tension distilled into a joystick.

    Kaboom! — If you have paddles, this is mandatory.

🎯 Great “Show Someone What the 2600 Is” Games

    Frogger

    Galaxian

    Demon Attack

    Berzerk

    Centipede (with trackball or joystick)

🔍 Deep Cuts & Underrated Gems

These are the games collectors love to show off — clever, unusual, or technically impressive.
🌀 Late‑Era Technical Flexes

    Solaris — Often considered the most advanced game on the system. Huge universe, smooth gameplay.

    Midnight Magic — Surprisingly good pinball physics for 2600 hardware.

    H.E.R.O. — A masterpiece. Multi‑tool gameplay, great pacing, and a cult favorite.

    Radar Lock — A pseudo‑3D flight shooter that feels like it shouldn’t be possible on a 2600.

🧪 Experimental / Weird / Ahead of Their Time

    Entombed — The maze generator algorithm is still studied today.

    Surround — A simple but addictive competitive game.

    Video Pinball — Early Atari magic; oddly hypnotic.

    Dark Cavern — Moody, tense, and surprisingly atmospheric.

🎨 Games With Personality

    Keystone Kapers — Fast, smooth, and charming.

    Plaque Attack — A shooter set inside a mouth. Yes, really.

    Private Eye — A detective game with a unique feel.

🕹️ If You Have Paddles (You Should)

The 2600’s paddle games are some of the best ever made.

    Warlords — Four‑player chaos. One of the best multiplayer games of the entire 8‑bit era.

    Kaboom! — Already mentioned, but worth repeating.

    Super Breakout — The definitive paddle showcase.

    Circus Atari — Pong meets breakout meets clown physics.

🏁 Late‑Era Activision & Atari “Hidden Gems”

These tend to be pricier but worth it.

    Beamrider — A fast, smooth shooter with a unique vibe.

    Seaquest — One of Activision’s most addictive games.

    Enduro — A surprisingly zen racing game.

    Jr. Pac‑Man — Better than the original 2600 Pac‑Man by a mile.
