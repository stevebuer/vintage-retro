🟩 1. The Brewing Academy (USA) — Easiest, Most Reliable Source

They sell new Atari 2600 PCBs, both simple and bankswitching types.
What they offer:

    2K/4K “standard” PCBs (perfect for diagnostic ROMs)

    F8/F6/F4 bankswitch PCBs

    E0, FE, and other exotic bankswitch boards

    Bare PCBs or full kits with sockets

Why they’re great:

    Small‑quantity friendly

    Boards are modern, clean, and well‑labeled

    Designed for EPROMs (27C32, 27C64, 27C128, etc.)

If you want a no‑hassle, drop‑in board for a diagnostic multi‑cart, this is the place.
🟦 2. AtariAge Store (USA) — High‑Quality Homebrew PCBs

AtariAge uses their own custom PCBs for homebrew releases, and they also sell blank boards when available.
Pros:

    Extremely high quality

    Support many bankswitching schemes

    Fit perfectly in standard shells

Cons:

    Stock varies

    Sometimes only available during certain production runs

Still, if you see them in stock, they’re excellent.
🟧 3. Console5 (USA) — Repair‑Grade PCBs

Console5 occasionally stocks new 2600 PCBs designed for repairs or repros.
Pros:

    Very clean manufacturing

    Good for simple 2K/4K ROMs

    Great documentation

Cons:

    Not always in stock

    Fewer bankswitching options

If you’re building a diagnostic cart, their simple boards are perfect.
🟨 4. PCBWay / JLCPCB (DIY Gerber Route)

If you want total control, you can order PCBs from a fab house using open‑source Gerbers.
Where to get Gerbers:

    GitHub (search “Atari 2600 PCB” or “2600 repro board”)

    AtariAge forums (homebrew dev section)

    Community projects like “2600 Repro Board”

Pros:

    Dirt cheap per board

    You can order 5–10 at a time

    Full control over EPROM footprint, switches, etc.

Cons:

    Requires sourcing Gerbers

    More DIY effort

    No components included

If you want to design your own multi‑cart PCB, this is the path.
🟫 5. eBay / Tindie (Independent Makers)

There are several hobbyists who sell small‑batch 2600 PCBs.
Pros:

    Often inexpensive

    Good for simple ROMs

    Easy to get a couple boards at a time

Cons:

    Quality varies

    Documentation may be minimal

Still a valid option if you want just one or two boards.
🎯 Which PCB Should You Get?

Since you’re talking about:

    burning your own ROMs

    possibly making a diagnostic multi‑cart

    wanting new boards, not donors

I’d recommend:
For a simple diagnostic cart:

    Brewing Academy 4K/8K standard PCB

For a multi‑cart with switches:

    Brewing Academy 27C256 or 27C512 multi‑ROM PCB  
    (lets you map multiple 2K/4K ROMs into one EPROM)

For a fancy bankswitching multi‑cart:

    AtariAge F8/F6/F4 boards (if in stock)
